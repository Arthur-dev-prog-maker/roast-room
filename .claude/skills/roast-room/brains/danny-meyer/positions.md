# Positions: Danny Meyer

Built from the four extract batches over 24 sources (S001 to S024). 36 topics. Each stance has verbatim quotes with source ids and dates from `sources.md`. Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position". Ordered roughly by how many sources touch the topic.

## Culture and stakeholder order
- **Stance:** Every business has the same five stakeholders and gets to choose their order: team, guests, community, suppliers, investors. It is a cycle, not a ranking, and investor profit is the outcome. Sources: S001, S004, S005, S007, S009, S010, S012, S014, S016, S017, S018 (11).
- "This is not a totem pole, it is a prioritization of our stakeholders, but it's a virtuous cycle. If you break it anywhere, you break the whole thing." (S018, 2024-08)
- "So if you really want to make money, the input should not be the investor." (S016, 2022)
- "culture’s what happens when you’re not in the room." (S009, 2026-09)
- **Over time:** staff first misread "we come first" as "what can Danny do for us"; he restated it as the team's job to look after each other. "It’s not my job to take great care of you. It’s your job to take care of each other." (S004, 2018-04). Newer framing: "Their first customer is each other." (S010, 2026-10)

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire 51 points for emotional skills (hospitality quotient) and 49 for technical skill; both halves are needed. Emotional skills are easier to spot in an interview than to change later. Sources: S001, S002, S004, S009, S013, S016, S017, S018, S021, S024 (10).
- "People need to be hired for their emotional skills 51% [of the time] and for their technical skills 49%." (S021, 2021-11)
- "we’re not going to hire someone who's great at fish if they don't also have a high HQ." (S017, 2026-09)
- "any organization will never be any better than the quality of the people that are on the team. Full stop." (S024, 2026-09)
- **Over time:** hiring only on the 51 left cooks who could not cook, so the target moved to both halves: "we want to get 100 on our test." (S009, 2026-09, newer)

## Opening new locations and concepts
- **Stance:** Grow where you are planted first. Open a second unit only when the first gets better too and a leader you trust is pulling for it. Copy only places people have made essential; keep "restaurants of terroir" single. Sources: S001, S002, S004, S008, S009, S011, S012, S016, S017, S020, S024 (11).
- "I’ll open a second restaurant if it can satisfy these three things: it’s even better than the first, the first one gets better in the process, and I’ll actually have a more balanced life in the process of opening this." (S004, 2018-04)
- "that was amongst the hardest leaps in my entire career, going from one to two" (S020, 2020-03)
- "the restaurants that did not work were the ones that really did not have my thumbprint on them." (S024, 2026-09)
- **Over time:** for ten years he would not open a second place, fearing his father's bankruptcies. "I didn’t want to end up like my dad." (S001, 2023-04). Later, on Shake Shack demand: "Who am I to hold back more of that?" (S009, 2026-09, newer). He now says "you don’t have a right to hold it back from people." (S008, 2024)

## Scaling a feeling across many locations
- **Stance:** Systems scale easily; the feeling is the hard part. Keep about 80 percent the same everywhere and make 20 percent local. Language is how culture travels. Sources: S002, S004, S009, S011, S012, S016, S021, S024 (8).
- "80% of the design, 80% of the menu is going to be the same everywhere, but 20% of the design and 20% of the menu should be customized to where we are." (S011, 2026-10)
- "can you scale relationships? Can you scale humanity?" (S012, 2026-10)
- "I have language, and I believe that language is the mortar between the bricks of culture." (S016, 2022)
- **Over time:** he says he was wrong to believe that "I felt that growing was going to be selling out." (S011, 2026-10). Newer: "What if it could actually get better as you grow?" (S024, 2026-09)

## Tipping and pay
- **2015 (ended tipping):** Hospitality Included removed tips so cooks, dishwashers and reservationists could share in pay and advance on merit. Piloted at The Modern, servers kept whole for three months. Sources: S001, S003, S019, S023.
  - "a major obstacle in this endeavor is the practice of tipping." (S023, 2015-10)
  - "we were doing a pretty good job of taking care of half of our team and a pretty awful job of taking care of the other half." (S019, 2021-11 replay)
  - "I don't want to hire people that would only be nice to you in the expectation that two hours later you're going to put some extra bucks on the tip tray." (S003, 2019-09)
  - Early results he cited: "doing the right thing is the most profitable thing." (S019) with the hedge "I’d say it’s probably too soon to tell, but so far, so good." (S019)
- **2020 (brought tipping back, newer):** In summer 2020 sidewalk guests tried to hand servers cash and he would not keep refusing it for staff taking Covid risk. Tipping resumed, and the company pays a nightly share of revenue to tip-ineligible cooks. Sources: S001, S010.
  - "this is inhumane, this is not being on our employees’ side." (S001, 2023-04)
  - "there just was not enough left for our investors." (S010, 2026-10)
  - "I think I learned that Americans like their tips." (S010, 2026-10)
- **Now:** still thinks the system is unfair and that tips multiply price rises into server pay, but does not think no-tipping would work better today. "There still has to be a better way." (S010, 2026-10) "I don't think there's a better shot of it working." (S010, 2026-10)
- **Read:** he will act on fairness at real cost (about $1 million in tip credits, S001), but the market, the staff, and the investor leg of the stool overruled him. Inferred from S001, S003, S010, S019.

## Menu pricing
- **Stance:** Prices should be all in, and the industry has been underpriced because it has not paid talent. Raise prices when suppliers raise theirs; margins leave no room. Sources: S003, S006, S007, S019, S020, S023 (6).
- "The American menu price structure is completely false, but we’ve been taught to believe that it’s real." (S019, 2021-11 replay)
- "we still have not persuaded the public to responsibly pay for talent." (S006, 2021-01)
- "we don’t have a choice because restaurants already have incredibly thin margins." (S007, 2022)
- He set Hospitality Included prices so the total check stayed about the same: "It’s the same math, but it’s a very different emotional calculus." (S019)

## Crisis layoffs
- **Stance:** Use the stakeholder order hardest in a crisis. Cut deep enough to survive, do it as kindly as possible, keep benefits, fund relief, and plan to rehire. Sources: S001, S005, S006, S007, S015, S017, S021, S024 (8).
- "how does this decision today increase our odds not only of surviving, but of being in an even better position when this comes out?" (S005, 2020-03)
- "But the kind thing to do was to do it one by one" (S017, 2026-09)
- "we have to end up being the best unemployer." (S024, 2026-09)
- **Facts he gives:** about 2,000 of 2,200 laid off in March 2020 (S005); later cuts down to 45 before rebuilding (S006, S007). "I wanted to do it all at once, but the goal post kept getting moved." (S007, 2022) Relief came from his own pay and 100 percent of gift card sales (S005, S024).

## Closing a restaurant
- **Stance:** Closing is not failure; keeping a place open from pride is the mistake. Close with the same style you opened: early notice, landlord paid, staff placed, community thanked. Sources: S001, S006, S010, S013, S024 (5).
- "closing a restaurant is not failing, and it’s also nothing to be ashamed of and nothing to be proud of to keep a restaurant open forever." (S001, 2023-04)
- "I kept it open for two years longer than I should have." (S001, 2023-04)
- Close when "We’re losing money, while we’re being unsafe, while we’re not making people happy" (S006, 2021-01)
- **Over time:** in Setting the Table (2006) he boasted of never closing one; now "I deeply regret that" (S024, 2026-09, newer). He still felt it personally: "I just felt like a complete failure for everything I had always worked at and believed in." (S010, 2026-10)

## Firing and underperformance
- **Stance:** Sort people by can and will. Coach the willing for months; act fast on the unwilling; integrity breaches go at once. A firing should never be the first conversation. Sources: S001, S009, S024 (3).
- "it shouldn’t be the first time you’ve had the conversation." (S001, 2023-04)
- "Is this something that you can do, but you won't? If so, we're going to give you a warning." (S024, 2026-09)
- Self-critique: "I was pretty damn bad and/or slow at exiting people who shouldn’t have been on the team." (S001, 2023-04)

## Company as family
- **Earlier:** USHG ran on "family values" until about 2022 (S018).
- **Newer (2024 to 2026):** "We are not a family. We’re a business." (S009, 2026-09) "We changed that to expected behaviors and it is a business." (S018, 2024-08) Sources: S001, S009, S010, S018. He admits "so I am full of contradictions." (S010)

## Landlords and leases
- **Stance:** Ask the landlord to be a partner in the neighborhood before discussing terms. Long leases signed before Covid rest on a dead premise, and rent spikes push out independents. Sources: S003, S005, S006, S010, S012, S015, S016, S024 (8).
- "Before we even talk about terms on the lease, will you join me?" (S016, 2022)
- "The premise on which restaurants have signed long-term leases is completely irrelevant today." (S006, 2021-01)
- "The landlord didn’t stop charging rent." (S005, 2020-03)
- On losing Union Square Cafe's lease when rent would triple: "do we really want to just become, you know, a bunch of big national chains everywhere?" (S010, 2026-10)
- He sorts landlords into partners, "tough luck" (Blue Smoke closed), and relief now with a later revisit, which he calls rational (paraphrase, S006). Classic-restaurant test he quotes from Joe Baum: "one that that lives longer than its original lease." (S024)

## Delivery and third-party platforms
- **Stance:** Thin evidence. He treats delivery as a tool and a crisis bridge, not the core. Shake Shack's digital head start let it reopen first in Covid; full-service was slow. He thinks people still want to go out. Sources: S002, S006, S015, S018, S020, S024 (6).
- "we did a really bad job of getting back into the business of being restaurateurs." (S015, 2022)
- "You don't have to go to a restaurant to get good food." (S020, 2020-03)
- Pizza at Marta did not travel, so he shut delivery and built a calzone virtual brand: "whoever wrote the rule that we can’t create our own virtual brand within one of our kitchens?" (S006, 2021-01)
- **No recorded position** on DoorDash or Uber Eats fees, markups, or exclusivity. He mentions DoorDash only to say people do not want every meal delivered (S018).

## Drive-thru
- **Earlier (2004 or 2005):** no drive-thru at Shake Shack.
- **Newer (2022):** "boy, was I wrong." (S015, 2022) About 8 to 10 drive-thru units and growing.

## Technology and AI
- **Stance:** Use tech behind the scenes to cut waste and wait; keep it out of the human moment. Sources: S003, S007, S010, S014, S022 (5).
- He invests in PreciTaste, camera-based order prediction, which "cuts way down on waste, which increases the productivity of the team, and which decreases the time you have to wait for your food." (S007, 2022)
- "That's not something that an AI agent is going to do. That's not something a robot is going to do." (S014, 2026-09)
- "I will never say never to anything, but I'll come real close when it comes to putting robots in our dining rooms." (S010, 2026-10)
- "Because so many of us are confusing tech with touch." (S022, 2026-10)

## Going public
- **Stance:** Never planned a chain and was reluctant, but values the IPO because staff could buy stock and build wealth. IPO came in 2015 at about 63 to 65 stores (S011). Sources: S008, S011, S017, S024 (4).
- "It was never my goal to have this be a chain." (S024, 2026-09)
- "it was a wealth creator for people." (S008, 2024)
- He keeps the separate company on the same values: "restaurants are restaurants." (S005, 2020-03)

## Raising money and investors
- **Stance:** Take outside money before family money so you learn real responsibility to investors. Spend little on a first venture. Ask investors for patience in a bad year. Sources: S003, S005, S007, S010 (4).
- "the less money you need to spend for your first venture, the better." (S003, 2019-09)
- "that was a crappy year for profits, but here we are and we’ve got a workforce and we’ve got a business." (S005, 2020-03)
- "if I really want to take care of my investor and I believe in a virtuous cycle, why would I put them first?" (S010, 2026-10)

## Investing in other founders
- **Stance:** Back leaders over ideas, at growth stage, when the culture puts investors as the outcome. Sources: S003, S007, S008, S016 (4).
- "I’ve never seen a great idea overcome bad leadership." (S008, 2024)
- "could they become the Shake Shack of their category?" (S007, 2022)

## Debt and bankruptcy
- **Stance:** Fears it from his father's history, but reads the cause as the wrong team, not growth. Post-Covid debt is a trap that suppliers and landlords will not extend. Sources: S005, S006, S008, S016.
- "Mostly that had to do with scaling with the wrong team." (S005, 2020-03)
- "So, what a lot of restaurants are stuck with now is even more debt." (S006, 2021-01)

## Margins and restaurant economics
- "A restaurant is basically a manufacturing company with a sales room attached to it." (S005, 2020-03)
- "on a good day for most restaurants is a 10% margin. That’s a good day." (S005, 2020-03)
- "We were founded on two principles that are no longer relevant. One is cheap rents, and the other was cheap labor." (S006, 2021-01)
- Sources: S005, S006, S007, S020.

## Discounting, comps and perks
- **Stance:** Comps and generosity cost less than losing a regular; the 51 percent staff dining discount is his best retention tool. A perk only helps if the person can use it. Sources: S016, S018, S019.
- "I expect that you believe that you’re never going to put us out of business by being generous to people." (S016, 2022)
- "And I will tell you that it has been the most potent retention tool ever." (S018, 2024-08)
- Low-paid cooks on an early dining discount: "I can’t even afford to pay my rent, so thanks a lot for giving me a discount on something I can’t afford anyway." (S019)
- **No recorded position** on price promotions such as BOGO or happy-hour discounting.

## Mistakes and guest recovery
- **Stance:** Handle mistakes so well the guest tells the story: aware, acknowledge, apologize, act, apply additional generosity. Honest mistakes are fine; dishonest ones are not. Sources: S002, S011, S014, S021, S022 (5).
- "As long as your mistake doesn't lack integrity, make it." (S021, 2021-11)
- "What if you could actually end up in a better spot because of how you handled the mistake, than if the mistake had never happened in the first place?" (S011, 2026-10)

## Leadership and delegation
- **Stance:** Surround yourself with people better than you, delegate what they do as well, micro care instead of micromanaging. Sources: S002, S007, S008, S009, S016, S018, S021, S024 (8).
- "I was going to constantly surround myself with people who could do things better than I could." (S008, 2024)
- "needs to stop micromanaging, but needs to double down on micro caring." (S009, 2026-09)
- "There’s no revenue in the home office. You’re a cost unless you are helping restaurants be more successful." (S009, 2026-09)

## Partners
- **Stance:** A great operator pulling for the idea is what unlocked each growth step (Colicchio for Gramercy Tavern, Garutti for the second Shack). Sources: S004, S008, S009, S020, S024.
- "I just really, really loved his cooking." (S020, 2020-03)

## Succession and stepping back
- Handed CEO to Chip Wade in 2022 and became executive chairman. "I wish I could have done probably two or three years sooner." (S007, 2022) "The hardest choice for me was really a foolish ego choice" (S011, 2026-10). Sources: S007, S011, S016.

## Brand and differentiation
- **Stance:** Food is table stakes; how guests feel is the brand. Aim for essential, not just long-lived. Sources: S001, S002, S011, S012, S016, S017, S021 (7).
- "having great food is table stakes; that what really, really matters even more is: how did we make you feel while we were creating that experience?" (S011, 2026-10)
- "Bold and remarkable wins the race." (S021, 2021-11)
- "Your favorite restaurant is the restaurant that loves you the most. End of story." (S016, 2022)

## Niche and positioning
- On a crowded category: "we just want to be your second-favorite version of everything we do here because we’ll never be as good as mom or grandma." (S016, 2022)
- A small menu earns each slot: "The ticket to get on that menu is you better be an amazing version of whatever that category is." (S002, 2020)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Competitive and proud of it; out-behave rivals and beat your own best. Sources: S001, S009, S016, S018, S022, S024 (6).
- "the businesses that out behave the competition are the ones that prevail." (S018, 2024-08)
- "The way you make people feel is not an excuse to not win." (S018, 2024-08)
- "I love to win, and I love to try to exceed my own personal best" (S022, 2026-10)

## Metrics and reviews
- Three-question check: "Does it feel better to work here today than it did last time we asked? Does it feel better to dine here today? And are we making more money?" (S018, 2024-08)
- He admits: "I'd love to find a way to measure what we call hospitality quotient." (S018)
- "a bad review doesn't really put you out of business anymore." (S024, 2026-09) The cost is team morale and keeping talent.
- Sources: S009, S016, S018, S024.

## Timing
- Says the best time to start is after a shock, when rents are reset and you owe no one: "this could be the greatest time ever to get into the business." (S006, 2021-01)
- "entrepreneurs need problems in the same way that surfers need waves." (S007, 2022)
- Do not coast at the top: "that’s the time you’ve got to break the glass and you’ve got to start over." (S001, 2023-04)

## Suppliers
- "It’s never going to be any better than the worst ingredients you put into it." (S001, 2023-04) He worried about small artisan suppliers in Covid (S005). Supply contracts were being repriced in 2022 (S007).

## Community and giving
- Shake Shack's first kiosk was gifted to Madison Square Park, which became landlord and gets a share of sales. "it lives somewhere between capitalism and philanthropy." (S011, 2026-10) Each Shack gives to a local nonprofit (S012). Sources: S003, S011, S012, S016, S017, S024.

## Remote work
- 90 percent of his staff cannot work remotely; the home office got open hours with no agenda. "people like being with people" (S018, 2024-08)

## Work hours and burnout
- Moved to a five-day week about three months into Union Square Cafe: "people didn't think I was tired, they thought I was angry." (S022, 2026-10) Says work-life balance was impossible early and prefers balancing body, mind, heart and spirit (S016).

## Focus versus diversification
- Opening two restaurants weeks apart was his worst management call: "that became the hardest thing I ever did." (S012, 2026-10) Inferred from S004, S012, S016: one new thing at a time, after the current one is strong.

## Business and politics
- "I think business is politics." (S007, 2022) Take public stands only if they match company values (S007). He required staff and guest vaccination ahead of the city (S007, S010).

## Paid ads, content, side projects, selling the company, equity splits
- **No recorded position.** Nearest: marketing earns "Mindshare is the outcome of great marketing." (S017) while hospitality earns heart share; he gifts Seth Godin's book on belonging (S001).
