# Frameworks: Danny Meyer

Built from the four extract-note batches over S001 to S024 (interviews, podcasts, one letter). 22 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated thresholds and habits, not quotes.

---

## 1. Enlightened hospitality (five stakeholders, virtuous cycle)
- **What it is:** Every business has the same five stakeholders and gets to choose their order: team first, guests second, community third, suppliers fourth, investors fifth. It is a cycle, not a ranking: each is the input to the next, and investor returns are the outcome. Named after the Gramercy Tavern salmon complaint (S004, S009, S016).
- **Parts:** Staff take care of each other first ("their first customer is each other"); that energy reaches guests; guests and staff lift the community; fair dealing keeps suppliers; profit follows. Break it anywhere and the whole cycle breaks. A vicious cycle runs the same way in reverse (S010, S018).
- **When to use:** Any decision that trades one stakeholder against another: pay, layoffs, price rises, leases, closing a unit, taking on investors. He says it matters most in a crisis (S005). He also uses it as an investment filter for Enlightened Hospitality Investments (S003, S007).
- **How to score:** Walk the decision through all five in order. Strong if it is good for the team and the guest and the money follows; weak if the investor is the input ("why would I put them first?"); fail if it breaks any one link badly, since he says one break breaks the whole thing.
- **Quote:** "This is not a totem pole, it is a prioritization of our stakeholders, but it's a virtuous cycle. If you break it anywhere, you break the whole thing." (S018)
- **Also:** "So if you really want to make money, the input should not be the investor." (S016)
- **Sources:** 13 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S007, S009, S010, S012, S014, S016, S017, S018, S024)
- **Change over time:** For about two years staff misread "we come first" as "what can Danny do for us"; he restated it as the team's job to take care of each other (S004, 2018; S018, 2024).

## 2. 49/51 recipe (51 percenters, now 100 percenters)
- **What it is:** Every job is scored out of 100. Technical performance (what you did) is worth at most 49 points. Hospitality performance (how you made everyone feel while doing it) is worth 51. Either half alone is a failing grade. He credits the 51 percent idea to Rich Melman (S003).
- **Parts:** Hire on both halves; review on both halves; promote people who are strong on both. At Shake Shack he hires on the 51 because milkshakes are quick to teach; at The Modern culinary skill comes first (S011).
- **When to use:** Hiring, performance reviews, promotions, and judging any guest-facing change ("is this the 49 or the 51?").
- **How to score:** 100 is the target. A plan that only improves the 49 (faster tickets, better recipe) and ignores how people feel scores no better than a pass on half the test. A hire who is great at fish but low on hospitality is a no (S017).
- **Quote:** "If you do all that stuff perfectly in our business you get a 49, which is a failing grade." (S003)
- **Also:** "our recipe is 49 parts technical prowess and 51 parts hospitality skills." (S009)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S009, S011, S012, S016, S017, S018, S021, S024)
- **Change over time:** Newer view (S009, 2026-09): hiring only 51 percenters left cooks who could not cook, so the goal is now "100 percenters" who score on both halves.

## 3. Hospitality quotient (HQ) and the six emotional skills
- **What it is:** HQ is how much happier a person is when they make someone else feel better. It comes from six emotional skills: kind optimism, curiosity, work ethic, empathy, self-awareness, integrity. He calls them emotional skills, not soft skills, because "soft skills are hard" (S013).
- **Parts:** Screen for the six in the interview because they are hard to change later. Interview moves: ask the biggest misperception others have of you (not "are you self-aware?"), ask what happened before age 12 that shaped you, ask for a charitable assumption (S017, S018). The cookie test: give a child the chips and see if the joy is in making and sharing (S013).
- **When to use:** Any hiring, promotion, or "who should run the new location" question.
- **How to score:** He would hire a learner over a know-it-all. Low HQ is a no for guest-facing work regardless of skill; he says some people should not be in the industry because serving does not make them happy (S013). He warns that his own high HQ biases him toward liking candidates, so he "parks" it in interviews (S017).
- **Quote:** "We're not interested in hiring know-it-alls. We love hiring learn-it-alls." (S009)
- **Also:** "we're much better at identifying those when we interview than we are at changing them once somebody's actually on the team." (S009)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S009, S013, S017, S018, S021, S024, S003)
- **Limit:** He says he does not know how to measure HQ (S018).

## 4. Use your words (language is the mortar of culture; expected behaviors)
- **What it is:** Culture scales through shared language. Name what was intuitive so it becomes teachable and intentional, then repeat it until people roll their eyes. The CEO must be the most fluent speaker.
- **Parts:** Short named behaviors drawn from real stories ("center the salt shaker"); shorthand acronyms (CDR, caught doing right; HWDB, how we do business); use the words in hiring, reviews and every meeting; hold people accountable to them. In about 2022 he replaced generic "family values" with expected behaviors (S009, S018).
- **When to use:** Opening a second unit, a culture that "feels different" at a new location, any rollout where staff will have to make choices without you.
- **How to score:** Ask whether the founder can state, in words, how staff should make the choice. If the plan relies on "they will see me do it", score it low: he ran two restaurants that way and failed (S016, S018).
- **Quote:** "I have language, and I believe that language is the mortar between the bricks of culture." (S016)
- **Also:** "it's not fair to ask your team members to be guessers as to what really matters." (S009)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S004, S009, S012, S013, S016, S017, S018, S021)

## 5. Hospitality versus service
- **What it is:** Service is the technical delivery of the product and is the same for everyone. Hospitality is the emotional transaction, tailored to one person, and turns on the preposition "for": did you feel they were on your side? You need both.
- **Parts:** Getting what you ordered is expected, not hospitality; remembering and customizing is hospitality. The food's arrival is the "sacred moment" where the two meet (S013). The golden rule of hospitality: do unto others as they would want, not as you would want (S009, S017).
- **When to use:** Any guest-experience, tech, kiosk, delivery, or staffing-cut decision. Ask which half it touches.
- **How to score:** A change that improves service but strips out the human, tailored part is a net loss in his frame. A change that frees staff to customize scores well.
- **Quote:** "hospitality describes an emotional transaction whereas service describes a technical transaction" (S004)
- **Also:** "It's one size fits one. Service is one size fits all." (S017)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S004, S009, S013, S017, S021)

## 6. Hospitality Included (no tipping) *(changed)*
- **What it is:** Menu prices include service; tipping goes away. Built to close the pay gap between tipped servers and cooks, who by law cannot share tips, and to give staff a merit career path.
- **Parts (2015 to 2016 rollout):** Announce to guests and hold a town hall (S023); pilot at The Modern first, then Maialino (S019); raise kitchen pay about 20 percent; guarantee servers their old pay for at least three months in writing; set prices so the check lands about where it was; do the math from known numbers (average gratuity was 21 percent) (S019).
- **When to use:** Pay structure, tip pools, service charges, back-of-house retention.
- **How to score:** He treats it as a three-legged stool: guests, staff, and investors must all come out feeling good (S001, S003). Score down any version where one leg is short.
- **Quote:** "It's the same math, but it's a very different emotional calculus." (S019)
- **Older view (2015 to 2019):** "a major obstacle in this endeavor is the practice of tipping." (S023) Early results looked good: "doing the right thing is the most profitable thing." (S019)
- **Newer view (2020 reversal, restated 2023 and 2026):** He brought tips back in 2020 (S001). In 2026: "there just was not enough left for our investors." (S010) and "I think I learned that Americans like their tips." (S010) He still wants a fix: "There still has to be a better way." (S010)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S007, S010, S019, S020, S023)

## 7. Five A's of mistake making (mistakes well handled)
- **What it is:** Be aware, acknowledge, apologize, act to fix it, apply additional generosity. The aim is to end up better off than if the mistake had never happened, so the guest tells the story of how well you handled it.
- **Parts:** The five steps, done faster each time. The three options in any mistake: pretend it did not happen, blame someone, or own it (S014). Mistakes come like waves; surfers are judged by not getting knocked off, not by taming the ocean (S011, S022).
- **When to use:** Guest recovery, a bad review, a failed launch, an operator error.
- **How to score:** Strong if the response owns it and adds generosity; weak if it is defensive or policy-first. He says a comp costs less than losing a regular (S016).
- **Quote:** "What if you could actually end up in a better spot because of how you handled the mistake, than if the mistake had never happened in the first place?" (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S011, S014, S016, S021, S022)

## 8. What could possibly go right?
- **What it is:** His daily question and the title idea of his 2026 book. After naming a problem and a fix, ask what could go right if you tried it. He says it does not promise things will go right.
- **Parts:** Start the day with it; keep worry for safety checks (S013); in planning, ask what happens if it actually works and whether you are ready for success (S018).
- **When to use:** A founder stuck in risk lists; a launch that may succeed beyond capacity (the first Shake Shack line).
- **How to score:** Upside planning is part of the score: a plan with no answer for "what if it works" is incomplete. He does not use it to excuse a weak plan.
- **Quote:** "a lot of our failures are when we got so caught up in the what could go wrong stuff that we failed to see, guys, what if this thing actually works, will we be prepared for success?" (S018)
- **Also:** "there's nothing about that expression that says that everything will go right." (S022)
- **Sources:** 5 (S013, S014, S018, S022, S024)

## 9. Essentiality test
- **What it is:** The goal is not long life but essentiality: a place that makes people's lives better because it exists, and would be missed if it closed. Hospitality is the means, not the end.
- **Parts:** Ask "is my life better because this place exists?" (S003). Joe Baum's older test, a classic restaurant outlives its original lease, is a floor, not the goal (S001, S024).
- **When to use:** Whether to keep, close, copy, or reposition a unit; whether a concept deserves a second location.
- **How to score:** Essential (people would miss it) is high; merely surviving is middling; kept open from pride is low.
- **Quote:** "the goal is to create an essential restaurant, a restaurant that actually, because of its very existence, made people's lives better." (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S011, S012, S024)
- **Limit:** "I think I've built restaurants that never achieved essentiality, but that's not what we strive for." (S002)

## 10. Restaurants of terroir versus paperbacks (grow where planted)
- **What it is:** Most of his places belong to the spot they were planted, like a single vineyard or a hardback book, and should not be copied. Some can become paperbacks and repeat. Make the paperback only once people clearly love the original and it is becoming essential.
- **Parts:** Grow where you are planted first; watch for real demand (the line); then you have no right to hold it back from people (S008). Opportunistic deals and other people's ideas tended to fail; places with his thumbprint worked (S024).
- **When to use:** "Should we open a second location / franchise / license this concept?"
- **How to score:** Yes if the original is loved and the demand is visible and growing; no if the reason is that a deal or a space came up.
- **Quote:** "Most of the restaurants we opened, I call restaurants of terroir." (S008)
- **Also:** "the restaurants that did not work were the ones that really did not have my thumbprint on them." (S024)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S008, S024, S009)

## 11. Community wealth (between capitalism and philanthropy)
- **What it is:** From Billy Shore's The Cathedral Within: use a business to build assets for the community. Shake Shack began as a hot dog cart to fund Madison Square Park; the kiosk was gifted so the park became the landlord and takes a share of sales.
- **Parts:** Before signing a lease, ask the landlord to partner on the neighborhood (S012, S016); tie each new Shack to a local nonprofit (S012); pursue the purpose, not success (S003).
- **When to use:** Site selection, landlord talks, a new neighborhood, local marketing.
- **How to score:** Higher when the unit gives the neighborhood a reason to want it there; lower when it extracts and leaves.
- **Quote:** "it lives somewhere between capitalism and philanthropy." (S011)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S011, S012, S016, S017)

## 12. Collect dots, connect dots (ABCD)
- **What it is:** Always be collecting dots so you can always be connecting dots. A dot is a morsel of information that matters to the other person. He reads every reservation report nightly (S001).
- **Parts:** Collect guest, staff, and supplier details; connect them into a gesture (seat two people near each other, stock the guest's book). The same move drives innovation: connect a stored taste memory to a new context (S016, S020), "write a new chord with the same existing notes" (S008).
- **When to use:** Guest data, CRM and reservation notes, menu or concept development.
- **How to score:** Strong if the plan captures what guests care about and gives staff a way to act on it; weak if data is collected but never turned into a gesture.
- **Quote:** "ABCDE simply means always be collecting dots so you can always be connecting dots" (S002)
- **Also:** "I think innovation isn't about inventing anything. It's about connecting dots" (S020)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S008, S014, S016, S020)

## 13. Crisis compass *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A short set of questions for a shock: does this decision raise our odds of surviving and of being better on the other side? Is this the thing that ends the world? Who were we while it happened, and who do we want to be after?
- **Parts:** Plan as if no help is coming (S005); communicate reality and hope together (S006, S009); treat a shutdown as dry dock to scrape the hull (S024); emotion first, then economics, then a price incentive (the 9/11 restaurant week) (S020); if you cannot be an employer, be the best unemployer (S024).
- **When to use:** Layoffs, forced closures, cash crunches, sudden demand drops.
- **How to score:** Strong if it protects people first and leaves the company stronger after; weak if it hoards cash by throwing away talent without care.
- **Quote:** "how does this decision today increase our odds not only of surviving, but of being in an even better position when this comes out?" (S005)
- **Also:** "that's my job as a leader, is to constantly communicate reality and hope." (S006)
- **Sources:** 6 (S005, S006, S009, S020, S021, S024)

## 14. Micro caring and the charitable assumption
- **What it is:** When visiting a unit, stop micromanaging and double down on micro caring: ask managers by name what they are proud of, ask from curiosity, never play gotcha. Open any correction with a generous read of the situation.
- **Parts:** Only correct a restaurant that does not report to you for danger or customers being lost that minute (S009). The Chicago Shake Shack story: he said the day looked busy before naming the mess (S009).
- **When to use:** Multi-unit oversight, owner visits, district manager roles.
- **How to score:** Strong if the oversight plan builds trust and lets local leaders own fixes; weak if it is a list of gotchas.
- **Quote:** "needs to stop micromanaging, but needs to double down on micro caring." (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S009, S017, S024)

## 15. Excellence, not perfection (journey of excellence)
- **What it is:** Perfection does not exist and is a recipe for unhappiness. Each day, honor yesterday's flawed work and pick two or three things to do a little better.
- **When to use:** Standards, opening checklists, a founder who will not launch until it is perfect.
- **How to score:** Favors steady daily improvement and honest review over grand fixes.
- **Quote:** "I think perfection is stupid, it's impossible, it's a recipe for unhappiness." (S001)
- **Also:** "Every single day of this crisis we've honored the work we did yesterday and found all the ways we could have done it better knowing what we know today." (S005)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S005, S008, S016)

## 16. Use growth to advance culture (culture is the soil)
- **What it is:** From Erika Andersen: do not try to "maintain" culture while growing; ask how growth can make it better. A growing business needs funding and leadership, and also soil, which is the culture. You can sell up, not sell out.
- **When to use:** Expansion, new units, hiring sprees.
- **How to score:** Strong if growth creates promotions and new reasons to live the behaviors; weak if culture is assumed to travel on its own.
- **Quote:** "how can we use our growth to advance our culture?" (S004)
- **Also:** "It needs the soil. And the soil is the culture." (S017)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S011, S017, S024)
- **Change over time:** He long believed growth meant selling out and that it caused his father's bankruptcies. Newer view (2024 to 2026): the real cause was surrounding yourself with the wrong team, and "you can do it all" (S008, S012).

## 17. The 80/20 rule (scaling a feeling across locations)
- **What it is:** At Shake Shack about 80 percent of design and menu is the same everywhere and 20 percent is local, often with a local charity tied to one item. The 20 percent is how a chain shows interest in a place.
- **Parts:** Send a team to eat at the best local places before opening; use local materials (the Yale Bowl seats in New Haven) (S002, S011).
- **When to use:** Rolling a concept to new neighborhoods or cities.
- **How to score:** Strong if the new unit is recognizably the brand and also clearly of its neighborhood; weak if it is a pure copy.
- **Quote:** "80% of the design, 80% of the menu is going to be the same everywhere, but 20% of the design and 20% of the menu should be customized to where we are." (S011)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S011, S012)

## 18. House pride (sibling rivalry)
- **What it is:** Each restaurant keeps its own pride and competes with the others, inside shared core behaviors. Siblings compete while loving and teaching each other.
- **When to use:** Multi-unit groups where one location's micro culture is drifting or a flagship looks down on newer units.
- **How to score:** Healthy if rivalry runs inside common behaviors; step in when micro cultures turn destructive (S009).
- **Quote:** "We call House Pride and we actually create opportunities for them to compete with each other." (S018)
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S016, S018)

## 19. Three conditions for a second restaurant
- **What it is:** He would open a second only if: it is even better than the first, the first gets better in the process, and his life is more balanced. He built it as a fail-safe against his father's path and failed all three at Gramercy Tavern.
- **When to use:** Going from one unit to two, which he calls among the hardest leaps of his career (S011, S020).
- **How to score:** Use as a stress test, not a gate he passed. If the first unit runs on the founder at the front door, expect it to slip; plan the words and leaders first.
- **Quote:** "I'll open a second restaurant if it can satisfy these three things: it's even better than the first, the first one gets better in the process, and I'll actually have a more balanced life in the process of opening this." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S011, S012, S016, S020)

## 20. Can and will quadrants
- **What it is:** For anyone not working out, plot ability (can or cannot) against willingness (will or won't). Can and will: celebrate. Cannot but will: coach, with a long wick (about six months). Can but won't: warning, short clock. Cannot and won't: out. Mirrors with the quadrants hung in locker rooms (S001, S024).
- **When to use:** Underperformers, firing decisions, a manager everyone is waiting on you to act on.
- **How to score:** Fast action on "won't"; patience on "will". An integrity breach skips the quadrants (S001). A firing should never be the first time the person hears the problem.
- **Quote:** "the wick on my candle is pretty long for someone who will." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S024)

## 21. Culture equation *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Culture is the sum of the behaviors you celebrate minus the behaviors you tolerate. Every compromise on a hire or promotion tells everyone your words were not meant (S017).
- **When to use:** Keeping a high performer with bad behavior; promotions.
- **How to score:** Tolerating a toxic star scores low no matter the sales.
- **Quote:** "the culture you have in your organization is the sum of all the wanted behaviors that you celebrate, minus all the unwanted behaviors that you tolerate." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S017)
- **Change over time:** He used to count only what he rewarded; he says he learned to count what he tolerates "the hard way" (S001, 2023).

## 22. Two pockets and who wrote the rule *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Two quick checks. Two pockets: one holds the truly hard problems, the other the ones where you know the answer but it is unpleasant; when the second fills up you are not doing your job (S007). Who wrote the rule: when a channel closes or a category has fixed habits, ask who decided you could not do otherwise (the Calzone Project virtual brand; Tabla breaking Indian cooking rules) (S006, S020).
- **When to use:** A founder delaying a known call; a closed channel; a concept boxed in by category habits.
- **How to score:** Delay on a known-answer problem scores low. Breaking a category rule scores well if it still serves the guest.
- **Quote:** "when that right pocket starts to get full, you are not doing your job because those problems, they only get more unpleasant as time goes by." (S007)
- **Also:** "whoever wrote the rule that we can't create our own virtual brand within one of our kitchens?" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S007, S020)
