# Positions: Nick Kokonas

Built from notes on 12 public sources (2011 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages. S010 and S011 are the same 2018 essay and count as one source.

32 topics, most-used first. Each stance carries source ids and dates (see `sources.md`). Where his view shifted, both versions sit side by side and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Competition and incumbents
- **Stance:** A monopoly that stops innovating is a target, not a stop sign. Copycats who compete on price or free lose. Judge a rival by how its money works, and count its customers yourself.
- "There is no such thing as a free lunch." (S003, 2025-10), on a rival paying restaurants $10,000 cash to leave Tock.
- "We will import your data, but not your pain." (S012, 2018-03)
- He tested Resy and Reserve claims by counting client lists and comparing revenue to capital burned (S010, 2018-11). An expert is vulnerable to a younger non-expert; quit before you are beaten (S006, 2026-10).
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S010, S012, S018).

## Dynamic and time-slot pricing
- **Stance:** The same seat at a different time is a different product. Price by day and hour, move prices both up and down, and reset them as fast as you can. He rejects "surge" as the word and as the model.
- "Tuesday is not Saturday" (S004, 2020-12)
- "Pricing should move in two directions" (S010, 2018-11)
- "it's not about surge pricing" (S005, 2021-07)
- "Nothing's priced forever." (S006, 2026-10)
- **Over time:** consistent from 2016 (S017) to 2026 (S006). The newer framing (S006, 2026-10) adds speed: daily, hourly, or by the minute, and says a faster reset is the whole arbitrage.
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S009, S010, S017).

## Prepayment and deposits
- **Stance:** Match the booking type to scarcity. Free when supply beats demand, a small deposit when demand beats supply, full prepay only when demand is extreme. Money down works through sunk cost; a card on file does not.
- "When supply exceeds demand, reservations should be free." (S010, 2018-11)
- "When demand exceeds supply, restaurants should require a small deposit to reduce no-shows and food waste." (S010, 2018-11)
- "if people had paid for it, they would show up and they would show up at all costs." (S003, 2025-10)
- Deposits should be credited to the bill so they never feel like a cover charge (S017, 2016-02). Prepay also creates float: forward money in the bank (S004, 2020-12), plus clearing risk (S002, 2020-05).
- **Over time:** share of free bookings: about 98 percent for mid-price restaurants (S010, 2018-11); about 70 percent of Tock volume (S004, 2020-12); about 80 percent (S005, 2021-07, newer). The ladder itself never changed.
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S009, S010, S012, S017).

## Reservation platforms (OpenTable, Resy)
- **Stance:** OpenTable is a front door, not a discovery network. It charges per diner for guests who came from search and social, keeps the guest data, and sells a restaurant's excess demand to rivals. Resy and Reserve copied it and burned cash. He built Tock because nobody would build what he needed.
- He called OpenTable a "dinosaur" (S012, 2018-03) and compared it to Comcast (S010, 2018-11).
- Rule from his Tock principles: never sell a restaurant's excess demand to its competitor (S010, 2018-11).
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S004, S005, S006, S009, S010, S012, S017).

## Margins and working capital
- **Stance:** Restaurants are not a bad business when run well; a 2 percent margin is a skill problem. Fixed costs are known, so the last dollar in mostly drops to profit. Hold a cash reserve and never pay last month's bills with this week's sales.
- "the last money in is the money you keep" (S009, 2025-02)
- "You can’t take percentages to the bank." (S001, 2018-10)
- "If you can’t pay for it, you can’t afford it." (S002, 2020-05)
- "A gift certificate is a liability on your balance sheet." (S002, 2020-05)
- **Note:** his Alinea margin figures vary by source: about 10 to 12 percent rising to 20 to 25 (S001, 2018-10); 6 to 8 rising to 20 to 25 (S009, 2025-02); 7 to 8 first year and 18 to 20 at peak (S006, 2026-10, newest). The direction is the same; quote ranges, not a single number.
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S009, S017).

## Marketing, PR, and content
- **Stance:** No PR firm. Measure every channel. Publish the plan in public before opening, talk to customers directly, and choose one message and repeat it.
- "Innovation is not about obfuscating your fees or buying clients or press releases that inflate your numbers." (S010, 2018-11)
- The open-sourced Alinea plan drew a waitlist and the New York Times (S006, 2026-10; S009, 2025-02). He emailed two diners or bloggers each morning for ten years (S004, 2020-12). A Netflix feature drove 20 to 30 percent of diners on a given day (S007, 2023).
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S009, S010, S018).

## Technology and AI
- **Stance:** Build it yourself when vendors refuse. Technology serves hospitality. Software should guide the guest down one path. Software makes its money on payments, not the subscription fee.
- "Ideally all restaurant technology is in the service of better hospitality." (S017, 2016-02)
- AI: an enthusiastic user who says institutions adopt it slowly (S009, 2025-02). Newer view (S006, 2026-10): AI can be the "big dumb button" that runs daily pricing for owners who will not do it, and in-person experiences gain value as AI spreads.
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S006, S009, S010, S017).

## No-shows
- **Stance:** A no-show is a yield leak. Multiply it by every night of the year. Count partial seatings and late cancels, or your numbers flatter you. Money down fixes it.
- "whenever you make a restaurant reservation, one of the two of you talking is lying to each other." (S001, 2018-10)
- "once they had $5 in for their reservation as a deposit, it took the no-show rate from 14 percent to under three percent." (S003, 2025-10)
- "Holding a credit card and charging after the fact simply does not work." (S012, 2018-03)
- Tock 2017 data: 8.04 percent ordinary, 3.67 deposit, 0.89 prepaid (S012, 2018-03). Cost to Alinea: over a million dollars a year (S004, 2020-12) or about $580,000 (S009, 2025-02, newer); the figures differ.
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S009, S012, S017).

## Timing
- **Stance:** Act early on small-probability, existential risks. Ship on day one in a new arena. Sell while growth is still high. Plan slack, sell the rest.
- "doing nothing is a choice" (S002, 2020-05)
- "It’s not like every restaurant in America can close all at once." (S002, 2020-05). He said this years earlier to his CFO and admits it was wrong.
- He waited four and a half years on Tock before hiring (S004, 2020-12). He bets in-person experience gains value as AI spreads (S006, 2026-10).
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S009, S010).

## Menu and ticket pricing
- **Stance:** Price the experience, not the food. Price varies by slot; Alinea at 5 pm Wednesday ran roughly $80 to $100 a person below 8 pm Saturday (S001, 2018-10). If the price point must change, change the product first.
- "what we really did was sell experience" (S009, 2025-02)
- Next opened at $85 for twelve courses and sold every seat for a year (S004, 2020-12). COVID carryout had to hit $30 to $35, so the dish became short rib Wellington (S002, 2020-05). Asked if anyone should spend $300 on dinner, he said he does not know (S007, 2023).
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007).

## Reviews and awards (Michelin)
- **Stance:** Prestige is not a business. Measure where guests actually came from. Treat any rating as temporary. Stars were a stretch goal, not a revenue plan.
- "nothing changes in terms of your actual business" (S007, 2023)
- "At some point, Alinea will be demoted." (S007, 2023)
- "It’s sort of a no-man’s-land between one and three, which is a problem." (S007, 2023)
- Yelp: monetized reviews bred refund shakedowns; do not play (S010, 2018-11). Hitting Gourmet's best-restaurant goal in year one horrified him because the lodestar was gone (S001, 2018-10).
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S004, S006, S007, S009, S010).

## Labor and tipping
- **Stance:** Service included is the future. His restaurants dropped tipping in 2011, paid servers above $150,000, and gave a 401k match and fast-vesting benefits. Align incentives before asking staff for more volume.
- He put the cost at about $500,000 a year and credits it with retention (S005, 2021-07). He hired more people so staff could work four days at higher pay (S006, 2026-10).
- Self-reported limit: he is a poor people manager and dislikes HR (S006, 2026-10; S009, 2025-02).
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S005, S006, S009, S017).

## Metrics and measurement
- **Stance:** Multiply small losses by seven days and 52 weeks. Look at dollars, biggest items first. Measure attribution before paying for anything.
- "There’s no point in looking at the dimes if you’ve got something that costs $1,000. Start there." (S001, 2018-10)
- "every dollar spends the same. They’re all the same." (S003, 2025-10)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S007, S010, S012).

## Discounting and free
- **Stance:** Never give the product away to win share; free attracts customers who need free. Discounting a slow slot is different: an empty seat is an expiring option, worth selling at half price or below food cost.
- "if you give something away from free, the people that you attract are the people who need free" (S004, 2020-12)
- "looking everything as like an expiring option" (S005, 2021-07)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S005, S010).

## Takeout and delivery
- **Over time:**
  - 2018-11: percent-of-gross fees fit takeout and not the dining room. "Charging a percentage of gross works for sending a burger out the back door of the kitchen, but it won’t work in the dining room." (S010)
  - 2020-05 (newer): a 20 to 30 percent delivery fee is tolerable as extra income and egregious when it is your only revenue; Tock To Go charged 3 percent (S002). 2021-07: pickup beats delivery; he is no fan of last-mile economics (S005).
- Size the offer to the cost base: $3,500 a night saves a sandwich shop, not a $6.5M payroll (S002, 2020-05).
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S004, S005, S010).

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Run lean and capital efficient; he dislikes the word burn. A big cap table pushes a company into bad pricing or a fire sale. He put up about a quarter of Alinea's $2.2M himself, with investors he knew (S001, 2018-10). Tock raised from Origin Ventures after about a year (S004, 2020-12).
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S004, S010).

## Niche and target market
- **Stance:** Aim at small, high, hard targets with less competition. Start high end, with customers who make you famous, then move down. But a slice too small will not sell out.
- "I always try to look for the high, small hoops." (S001, 2018-10)
- "you're going to have a hard time selling out" (S005, 2021-07)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S004, S005, S006).

## Purchasing and vendor terms
- **Stance:** Use cash to prepay concentrated vendors for a steep discount, and ask the vendor why a price is what it is. Beef quoted at $34 a pound dropped to $18 on a four-month prepay because of the vendor's aging cycle (S004, 2020-12). Prepay plus a cash reserve can move food cost from about 34 to 24 percent (S005, 2021-07).
- "you want to be a buyer when everyone in the world says that there is, it’s totally not worth buying." (S002, 2020-05)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S004, S005).

## Selling a company
- **Stance:** Sell while growth is high. Run diligence yourself. Skip the investment bank if you can do the work in three days.
- He offered his board a 2 percent fee instead of a banker's 3 to 4 and cleared an 896-question diligence list in three days (S006, 2026-10). Tock sold for about $430M after 8 years (S009, 2025-02). He has since left and watches from outside (S003, 2025-10). He sold the Alinea Group when the work stopped feeling like a puzzle (S009, 2025-02).
- **Date conflict:** S006 (2026-10) puts his Alinea Group exit in 2023; S009 (2025-02) says October 2024. This brain uses October 2024.
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S006, S009).

## Brand and naming
- **Stance:** Pick a message and repeat it ("fun and delicious" (S004, 2020-12)). Avoid words that scare buyers.
- **Over time:** 2016-02: he described "deposit tickets" credited to the bill (S017). 2018-11 (newer): calling them "tickets" in 2014 was his biggest Tock mistake, because diners and owners hear that they must pay first (S010).
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S010, S017).

## Hiring and first hire
- **Stance:** Tell hires the downsides so they can say no. Pay interns. Pick the person who understands the goal, even if busy. He waited four and a half years on Tock before hiring (S004, 2020-12).
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S006).

## Partners and investors
- **Stance:** Structure it cleanly: investor LLC, restaurant LLC, management company; investors paid for their meals (S004, 2020-12). Investors got quarterly distributions and about 28X total (S006, 2026-10). Align any agent's pay with your price: he gave house agents half of anything above a high target (S001, 2018-10).
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S006).

## Paid ads
- **Stance:** Only spend where you can measure. He sold books through his own list and ads (S004, 2020-12) and found Michelin's attributable lift low next to Netflix and Instagram (S007, 2023). Inferred from S001, S004, S007.
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S007).

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Several bookable experiences inside one venue, yes (the Gramercy Tavern test, S004, 2020-12). Keep crisis products customers still buy (S005, 2021-07). Opening Next was harder because he stopped watching what went smoothly before (S018, 2011-03). Inferred from S002, S004, S005, S018.
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S004, S005, S018).

## Culture and meetings
- **Stance:** Short written notes over meetings. Throw ideas out openly, kill the losers fast, and post-mortem daily.
- "it is the perview of every bad organization to schedule eight hours of meetings and zero hours of work" (S009, 2025-02)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S009).
