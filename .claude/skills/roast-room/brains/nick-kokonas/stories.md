# Stories: Nick Kokonas

Built from notes on 12 public sources (2011 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages. S010 and S011 are the same 2018 essay and count as one source.

17 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **The Next ticket launch.** Next sold prepaid seats on a home-built system run by one programmer and him, launched hours before the first service. The site took 8,000 to 9,000 visitors against 700 expected; he had no phone line on purpose. Sales in the first day are reported as $562,000 (S001), over $500,000 (S017), and about $80,000 (S009); the figures conflict, so give a range or cite one source by id. Lesson: demand was waiting behind a bad process, and removing the fallback forced the new model to work (S001, S004, S009, S017).
2. **The movie he did not want to see.** He would have paid $50 to skip a film, but once he had bought tickets he made the family go. The day he started Tock he resolved to put deposits on every Aviary guest. Lesson: sunk cost explains why money down cuts no-shows (S001, S003).
3. **The empty Wednesday.** Staff asked why Wednesday ran 74 covers with a 100-person waitlist. He opened 110 seats a day for two weeks, a manager pushed back on filling every day, and the manager left. He calls it his own failure to explain the incentive. Lesson: look at the empty seat and ask why, then fix the pay before asking for volume (S001, S006).
4. **Gramercy Tavern's five experiences.** Tavern bar, a la carte room, two tasting menus, private rooms, plus books and merchandise, and no way to book any of them before arrival. Lesson: people cannot buy what you are not selling (S004, S005).
5. **The trading-floor resume.** He deleted his degree to get a $5 to $5.25 an hour runner job, then paid a trader to teach him options. Lesson: get into the arena first and buy the education directly (S001, S006, S009).
6. **COVID read from reservation data.** Hong Kong bookings fell from 95 percent to zero, then Italy, then Seattle dropped 30 percent. He tracked payment flows by the hour and ordered shutdown plans on March 4, 2020, before Illinois closed restaurants. Lesson: act on small-probability existential risk early and use crowd data nobody knows they give (S002, S004, S009).
7. **Beef Wellington carryout.** Carryout had to hit $30 to $35 and travel, so tenderloin became short rib Wellington. 500 sold in five minutes, then 1,250 a night; he rehired about half the staff within a week. Tock To Go shipped in six days at 3 percent. Lesson: when overhead is high, a cheap repeatable product at volume beats an elaborate one (S002, S004, S005).
8. **The table-plate dessert.** A Picasso documentary led to a giant plate idea, then latex on a table-sized canvas; it took seven months and became a Netflix billboard. Lesson: let bad ideas stand long enough for one to become iconic (S004, S009).
9. **Hosts taking cash.** At one famous restaurant, maitre d's took cash tips to hold tables and fought the move to Tock. Lesson: look for who gains from the old system before you change it (S004, S005).
10. **Publishing advances.** Publishers offered $250,000 to $300,000 with a clause making the restaurant buy 2,500 books at half retail. A print broker put the real cost near $2.23 a book on a 30,000 run for a book near $60. He self-published and sold direct. Lesson: opacity is where the margin hides (S001, S004).
11. **The Alinea Project.** He posted the plan in public before opening, including rejected names and an essay on bread. It drew half a million views, a waitlist, and the New York Times critic. Lesson: build the audience before the doors open (S006, S009).
12. **The prepaid beef.** A beef quote of $34 a pound fell to $18 on a four-month prepay. He asked why and learned the vendor's aging cycle. Lesson: ask the vendor why the price is what it is (S004, S002).
13. **Michelin loses money on Chicago.** The guide's publisher told him it spent more dining at Alinea than the Chicago guide earned. Stars brought a week of news and no change in business. Lesson: prestige is not a business (S007).
14. **The word "tickets".** He regrets the word from his 2014 post; diners and owners hear that they must pay first, and rivals used it to paint Tock as scary. Lesson: the label can hide the product (S010).
15. **The dedicated phone line.** He ran a line between the New York and Chicago floors to cut a 30-second delay, after checking the rules did not forbid it. He took a little at a time and the year made over $100M for the firm. Lesson: read the rules, be patient, do not empty the pocket on day one (S006).
16. **Selling the house.** He priced it at a fair floor and offered agents half of anything 10 percent over. It sold in two days at about 40 percent higher. Lesson: fix the agent's incentive (S001).
17. **Passing on Impossible Foods.** He passed because he could not imagine people paying a premium. "I was completely wrong" (S005). Lesson: his own taste test blinded him to what others would pay (S005).
