# Principles: Nick Kokonas

Built from notes on 12 public sources (2011 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages.

18 decision rules, most-supported first. The Sources line counts distinct sources (ids listed); S010 and S011 are the same essay and count as one (written S010/S011). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Price both directions; lower the slow slot, do not only raise the busy one.** A price that can only rise is a surge fee and customers cry foul. "Pricing should move in two directions" (S010) "it's not about surge pricing" (S005) Sources: 7 (S001, S003, S005, S006, S009, S010/S011, S017). Confidence: high.

2. **Ask for money up front only where demand exceeds supply; keep the rest free.** For approachable mid-price restaurants about 98 percent of bookings stay free (S010). "When demand exceeds supply, restaurants should require a small deposit to reduce no-shows and food waste." (S010) Sources: 6 (S001, S004, S005, S009, S010/S011, S012). Confidence: high.

3. **Real money down beats a card on file; even $5 changes behavior.** "if people had paid for it, they would show up and they would show up at all costs." (S003) "Holding a credit card and charging after the fact simply does not work." (S012) Sources: 5 (S003, S004, S005, S012, S017). Confidence: high.

4. **Multiply small leaks by every night of the year before you dismiss them.** No-shows and short-seated tables at 5 to 8 percent of nightly revenue (S001) become a six- or seven-figure annual loss (S004, S009). Count partial seatings and late cancels honestly (S012). "whenever you make a restaurant reservation, one of the two of you talking is lying to each other." (S001) Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S009, S012, S017). Confidence: high.

5. **Do not give the product away or compete on price; free attracts people who need free.** "if you give something away from free, the people that you attract are the people who need free" (S004) He judged rivals who went cheap as a race to the bottom (S005) and saw a free-network strategy burn about $30M for about $1M of revenue (S010). Sources: 4 (S002, S004, S005, S010/S011). Confidence: high.

6. **Own the guest relationship; never let a platform sell your overflow to a rival.** "Your customers cannot be strangers." (S004) Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S006, S010/S011, S012). Confidence: high.

7. **Show the whole inventory and make every product bookable before arrival.** "people can't buy what you aren't selling" (S004) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S017). Confidence: high.

8. **Ask why the convention exists, and ask the vendor why the price is what it is.** Opacity is where margin hides (S001); the beef quote halved once he learned the vendor's aging cycle (S004). "It sounds like a dumb thing, but read the rules." (S006) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S006, S010/S011). Confidence: high.

9. **Hold a cash reserve and stay ahead of payables; prepay concentrated vendors for a discount.** Never let this week's sales pay last month's bills (S002, S004). "If you can’t pay for it, you can’t afford it." (S002) Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S005). Confidence: high.

10. **Run it as a business first so the art can be funded.** A profitable restaurant can reinvest in the guest (S006). "This will be run as a business first. It's not an art project." (S004) Sources: 4 (S004, S006, S009, S017). Confidence: high.

11. **Judge the decision process, not one outcome; take lopsided bets and cut losers fast.** "Always look for something that the upside is 3-4x the downside." (S001) "doing nothing is a choice" (S002) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S004, S009). Confidence: high.

12. **Measure where guests came from before paying for press or chasing awards.** "nothing changes in terms of your actual business" (S007) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S007, S010/S011). Confidence: high.

13. **Align incentives before asking staff for more volume.** A manager paid the same on a full Saturday has no reason to fill Wednesday; he says failing to explain it was his fault (S006). He traced empty seats beside a long waitlist to misaligned incentives (S001), and fixed agents' pay to beat a price target (S001). No verbatim quote in the notes. Sources: 2 (S001, S006). Confidence: medium.

14. **If the price point must change, change the product first, and size it to the cost base.** COVID carryout had to be $30 to $35 and travel well, so the dish changed (S002); $3,500 a night suits a sandwich shop, not a $6.5M to $7M payroll (S002). Keep the pandemic products guests still buy (S005). No verbatim quote in the notes. Sources: 2 (S002, S005). Confidence: medium.

15. **Gift cards and crowdfunding are liabilities, not rescue capital.** "A gift certificate is a liability on your balance sheet." (S002) "if you buy the gift certificate and then you go use it in four months, they spent that money four months ago." (S002) Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low.

16. **Name the product carefully; the label can sink adoption.** He calls the word "tickets" his biggest Tock mistake because diners and owners balk at paying first (S010). No verbatim quote in the notes. Sources: 2 (S010/S011, S017). Confidence: medium. Note: S017 (2016) still promotes "tickets"; S010 (2018) is the later view, so prefer it.

17. **Raise little and stay capital efficient; a big cap table forces bad pricing.** He dislikes the word burn (S004); a large cap table and urgency to return capital push a company into bad pricing or a fire sale (S010); rivals who gave the product away burned $60M to $70M then laid off half their staff (S002). No verbatim quote in the notes. Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S010/S011). Confidence: high.

18. **Sell a company while growth is still high, and run the process yourself.** Going from 200 to 50 percent growth makes a buyer see decline (S006). He refused an investment bank, offered the board a 2 percent fee, and ran an 896-question diligence list in three days (S006). He sold the group when the work stopped feeling like a puzzle (S009). No verbatim quote in the notes. Sources: 2 (S006, S009). Confidence: medium. Dates: S006 (2026) puts the Alinea Group exit in 2023; S009 (2025) says October 2024. This brain uses October 2024, from the more specific source.

## Where these rules fit a neighborhood operator badly
- Rules 2, 3 and 4 assume demand above supply. He says himself that pricing cannot be pressed hard when demand is below supply (S004) and that catering to a small slice of the population makes selling out hard (S005). For a casual bar the deposit ladder is a peak-night and holiday tool.
- The last-dollar math behind rules 4 and 10 assumes fixed, salaried labor (S009). Alinea ran about 85 staff for 64 seats at about $20M a year (S002) and eliminated tipping in 2011 (S005); hourly, tipped staffing keeps less of each extra dollar. Inferred from S002, S005, S009.
- Rules 5, 6 and 17 come from a founder who built and sold a venture-backed competitor to OpenTable (S009); S010 to S012 are self-interested and he says so (S010).
- He admits he is a poor people manager (S006, S009), so his staffing advice is thinner than his pricing advice.
