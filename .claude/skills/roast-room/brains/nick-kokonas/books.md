# Books: Nick Kokonas

Built from notes on 12 public sources (2011 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages.

Two books he co-wrote with Grant Achatz. No book text was read; the entries below describe only what the notes say about them, in my own words.

## Life, on the Line (2011, with Grant Achatz)
- **What it is:** Achatz's memoir, written by the two of them with no ghostwriter (S001). It covers Grant's stage 4 tongue cancer, diagnosed within about 18 months of Alinea opening (S009).
- **Core thesis as the notes describe it:** Kokonas writes as the everyman diner voice, and he put real investor updates and numbers in the book as a blueprint, because other industry books skip the numbers (S018).
- **Key frameworks in it:** publish real numbers rather than vague advice (S018); the business-first structure of Alinea (investor LLC, restaurant LLC, management company) is described in his talks but the notes do not say it is in the book (S004).
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S004, S009, S018.

## Alinea (the Alinea cookbook)
- **What it is:** The restaurant's cookbook, self-published; he says it was produced in a way similar to Modernist Cuisine (S018).
- **What the notes support:** The business case, not the recipes. Publishers offered $250,000 to $300,000 advances with a clause making the restaurant buy 2,500 books at half retail; a print broker put the real cost near $2.23 a book on a 30,000 run for a book retailing near $60 (S001). He self-published, used a distributor at about 27 percent of sale price with printing at cost, and kept the rights (S001). He sells his books directly through his own list, site, and ads (S004). Lesson he draws: opacity is where the margin hides, and own the channel (S001, S004).
- **Not covered:** The notes say nothing about the cookbook's content. The notes also mention The Aviary Cookbook in passing (S004), with no detail.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S004, S018.
