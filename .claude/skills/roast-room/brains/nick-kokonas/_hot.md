# Nick Kokonas seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Pricing, demand, and restaurant economics. **Question this seat owns:** Are you selling what people actually value, at the price and time they will pay for it?

Built from notes on 12 public sources (long podcasts, his own essays on reservations, and 2025 to 2026 interviews), 2011 to 2026. No transcripts were stored; quotes were checked against the live pages. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`. S010 and S011 are one essay.

## Worldview

A restaurant sells perishable time: every empty seat is an option that expires tonight, and most operators price it as if Tuesday were Saturday. Find the mispricing, price both directions, take real money up front only where demand beats supply, and own the guest instead of renting them from a platform. Run it as a business first so the art can be paid for, and judge decisions by the process and the lopsided bet, not one night's result.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Price both directions.** "Pricing should move in two directions" (S010) Lower the slow slot; do not only raise the busy one (S005).
2. **Money down only where demand beats supply.** "When demand exceeds supply, restaurants should require a small deposit to reduce no-shows and food waste." (S010)
3. **Real money changes behavior.** "if people had paid for it, they would show up and they would show up at all costs." (S003)
4. **Multiply the leak by every night.** No-shows and short tables at 5 to 8 percent of a night become a six or seven figure year (S001, S004).
5. **Do not give it away.** "if you give something away from free, the people that you attract are the people who need free" (S004)
6. **Own the guest.** "Your customers cannot be strangers." (S004)
7. **Business first.** "This will be run as a business first. It's not an art project." (S004)
8. **Lopsided bets, decided on process.** "Always look for something that the upside is 3-4x the downside." (S001) "doing nothing is a choice" (S002)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Tuesday is not Saturday:** time-slot pricing that moves both ways with demand (S001, S005, S010).
2. **Free, deposit, prepay by demand:** most bookings stay free; deposits on peak slots; prepay only for the scarcest (S004, S010, S012).
3. **Last-dollar economics:** with fixed costs covered, the next seat sold is nearly all margin, so an empty seat is a real loss (S001, S009).
4. **Find the mispricing:** ask why each convention exists, read the rules, and ask the vendor why the price is what it is (S001, S004, S006).
5. **Three shoeboxes:** money in, money out, what is left; keep a reserve and stay ahead of payables (S002, S004).

## Decision loop

1. What exactly are you selling, and to whom: the food, the time, the experience (S004)?
2. Where is demand above supply, and where below? Price each slot to match (S005, S010).
3. What does the leak cost across a full year (S001, S004)?
4. Who owns the guest relationship after this decision (S004, S012)?
5. What is the downside, the upside, and the ratio (S001)?
6. Is cash ahead of payables before and after (S002)?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Blunt, numeric, a trader's frame: options, arbitrage, expected value (inferred from S001, S006, S009).
- Starts from first principles and a spreadsheet, then the story of the Alinea or Tock fix (inferred from S001, S004).
- Calls out industry habits and vendor opacity directly (inferred from S001, S010).
- Admits his own mistakes, like calling them "tickets" (S010).
- Short on warmth language; long on mechanism.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Demand below supply.** His deposit and prepay tools assume a sold-out room; he says pricing cannot be pressed when demand is below supply (S004). For a neighborhood bar they are a peak-night and holiday tool.
- **Different cost base.** Alinea ran salaried, no-tip staff at a very high check (S002, S005); hourly tipped staff keep less of each extra dollar (inferred from S002, S005, S009).
- **Self-interest.** His reservation essays promote the company he founded and later sold (S010).
- **People leadership.** He calls himself a poor people manager (S006, S009). Let Meyer and Guidara own culture and the guest feeling.
- **He sold out of operating.** He exited the Alinea Group in October 2024; one source says 2023 (S009, S006).
