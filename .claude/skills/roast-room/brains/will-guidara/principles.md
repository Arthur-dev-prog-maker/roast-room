# Principles: Will Guidara

Built from notes on 13 public sources (2022 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages.

16 decision rules, most-supported first. The Sources count is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. A closing section lists where he is out of his depth.

1. **Relationships built through hospitality are the only durable competitive advantage.** Products and brands get matched; relationships are slow to build and slow to erode. "The only competitive advantage that exists over the long term comes through hospitality, through consistently investing in relationships" (S011) "hospitality and investing in relationships is the greatest competitive advantage any business can have." (S001) Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S007, S011, S017). Confidence: high.

2. **Give the front line permission and resources, then let them own it.** Fund the person and the budget that lets staff act on ideas, not just the speech; give pieces of the business to many people and let them take the credit and the press. "It was effectively like turning salespeople into product designers." (S002) "I found that the more trust you give people, the more trustworthy they become." (S004) Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S004, S011, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

3. **Judge a gesture by thoughtfulness, not cost.** A free first dance or a two-dollar hot dog counts as much as an expensive one; a comped bottle lacks specificity. "It's not about the cost: it's about the thoughtfulness." (S001) "it’s not the cost of the gesture that matters. It’s how it makes people feel." (S007) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S007, S017). Confidence: high.

4. **Earn the right to splurge, and never spend zero.** Hold 95% of the business to maniacal cost control so the 5% of generosity is sustainable; keep the 5% in hard times and play offense. "we manage our money like maniacs 95% of the time, such that we could earn the right to do this the other 5% of the time" (S014) "financially reckless not to be doing some of that foolish spending" (S019) Sources: 6 (S003, S004, S014, S016, S018, S019). Confidence: high.

5. **Take care of the team before the guest.** Staff cannot give a feeling they have not received, and staff are the most scalable lever. "I don't think it's possible to know how good it feels to give it until you first have experienced how good it feels to receive it." (S014) Sources: 5 (S004, S011, S014, S015, S016). Confidence: high.

6. **Fix excellence first; no gestures on a broken operation.** "excellence is merely table stakes." (S014) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S014, S017). Confidence: high.

7. **Hire for chemistry and keep the must-have list short.** Interview like a first date; the right person beats the most credentialed one. "We over-index on trying to hire for capacity and under-index on trying to hire for chemistry." (S001) "I try to pick the right people, not the best people" (S004) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S017, S018). Confidence: high.

8. **Hospitality can be trained, so train both it and excellence, and never stop.** He rejects the common hire-for-hospitality, train-for-excellence line; train all week like a football team. "I think you can train both." (S015) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S011, S015). Confidence: high.

9. **Use AI to make people more human, not to replace them.** Cutting staff for AI trades tomorrow's loyalty for today's savings; redeploy freed people to human work. "some companies will use AI to give humans the tools to be even more human, and some companies will use AI to replace their humans." (S004) "making today dollars at the expense of tomorrow" (S017) Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S017, S019). Confidence: high.

10. **Correct the behavior, privately, unemotionally, constantly; praise as often.** "Criticize the behavior, not the person." (S011) "if praise is affirmation, criticism is investment" (S016) Sources: 3 (S011, S016, S017). Confidence: high.

11. **Read the room: one size fits one only when it fits.** Do not force gestures on every table, and do not try to be all things to all people; some guests want you out of the way. "come up with one size fits one when it fits" (S002) Sources: 3 (S002, S015, S017). Confidence: high.

12. **Nothing in hospitality is hard; it takes trying harder.** He uses this to dismiss we-can't objections. "Nothing we did was hard. We were just willing to try harder." (S001) "Things that require that you try harder to achieve them, those things aren't hard." (S002) Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S019). Confidence: high.

13. **Choose connection over being right, and fund recovery.** When a guest is upset, who is right does not matter; a good repair makes the most loyal guests. "it's not about who's right or who's wrong, it's about how you made someone feel" (S018) "I think of customer recovery as one of the greatest forms of marketing you can do, and people woefully underspend on it." (S017) Sources: 2 (S017, S018). Confidence: medium.

14. **Efficiency alone makes a business forgettable and, in the end, less efficient.** "I think if a leader is only focused on efficiency, they will become less efficient." (S005) "if you are evenly distributing your focus on expenses, you’re going to have an efficient but completely unmemorable business." (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S005). Confidence: medium.

15. **Do right by the guest even when it costs revenue; no tricks.** Serve guests, not egos (give the ketchup), and give an honest way to leave or cancel even if more people cancel. "how you say goodbye is just as important as how you say hello" (S019) Sources: 2 (S002, S019). Confidence: medium.

16. **Hire slow, fire fast, but not too fast.** Give a struggling person real training or a better role first; one poor performer sets the reputation of the whole business. "hire slow, fire fast, but not too fast" (S005) "You can't identify a poor performer until you've given all of yourself to make them great." (S005) Sources: 1 (S005). Confidence: low.

## Where he is out of his depth

- **No hard ROI.** He says the return on hospitality is hard to measure and offers no metric, only the belief that it makes businesses more profitable (S011, S016, S017, S018, S019). The council should supply the numbers he does not.
- **His only concrete comp number is borrowed.** The UPS Store rule (each register worker comps one person a day, up to about $30) is a franchisee's, not his (S017). The 95/5 split and the "1% to 5%" range (S003) are the closest he gets to a budget.
- **Fine-dining economics behind the stories.** The Dreamweaver role, sleds in Central Park, a private-room beach, and extravagant gestures that needed hired help (S007) were funded by a high-check, high-margin room. A casual bar or counter-service shop cannot fund a no-duty headcount the same way (inferred from S001, S004, S007, S014).
- **Casual claims are argued, not operated.** He says the ideas apply to In-N-Out, Taco Bell, barbershops, and dentists (S014, S015, S011), and he ran a MoMA cafeteria concept (S003, S017), but nearly all his casual examples are other companies' (UPS, Chewy, Five Guys, an airline pilot). Treat his casual-ops advice as untested by him (inferred from S004, S014, S015, S017).
- **Staffing assumptions.** Entry-level hires who serve only after a couple of seasons (S001), a 30-minute daily pre-meal (S001, S018), and closing the restaurant for all-hands strategy days with about 150 staff (S017) assume low turnover and paid time a lean operator may not have (inferred from S001, S017, S018).
- **Risks he did not concede.** When a host raised that staff drift from basics toward magic and that guest expectations keep rising, he answered with the 95/5 rule and excellence-first but did not concede either risk (S014).
- **Self-reported weak spots.** He can be controlling and lose his temper, and fixes it by journaling and apologizing the next day (S017); consulting frustrated him because he cared too much (S019); he says yes too often (S017).
- **Distance from operating.** He sold EMP at the start of 2020 (S007) and was a week from signing three new leases when Covid hit (S015). His recent work is speaking, books, gatherings, and a creative studio (S019), so his downturn advice (S016, S018) comes from principle more than from running a restaurant through one.
