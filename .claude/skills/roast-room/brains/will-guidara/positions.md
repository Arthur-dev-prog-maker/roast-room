# Positions: Will Guidara

Built from notes on 13 public sources (2022 to 2026), with quotes checked against the live pages.

Operator topics first, then founder topics, most-used first within each group. Dates come from `sources.md`. S018 is dated 2026-10-28 but is a re-air of an October 2022 interview, so treat its views as 2022. Where a view changed, both versions sit side by side and the newer one is marked.

## Operator topics

## Comps and generosity budgets (the 95/5 rule)
- **Stance:** Run costs tight almost all the time so you earn the right to spend lavishly on a small slice that people remember. Not spending that slice is the real recklessness. Sources: S001, S003, S004, S007, S014, S016, S017, S019 (8).
- "I manage every expense like a freaking maniac 95% of the time." (S004, 2023)
- "we manage our money like maniacs 95% of the time, such that we could earn the right to do this the other 5% of the time" (S014, 2024-11)
- "financially reckless not to be doing some of that foolish spending" (S019, 2026-05)
- The 5% must go to staff as well as guests, or staff ask what is in it for them and it does not scale (S004, 2023). Protect the 5% in hard times too (S016, 2024-09).
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-09: a comped bottle or caviar lacks specificity; "it’s not the cost of the gesture that matters. It’s how it makes people feel." (S007)
  - 2026-05 (newer): he endorses a structured comp: a UPS Store franchisee let each register worker comp one person a day, up to about $30 (S017). Same idea, now with a cap and a rule.
- **Read:** cost is never the measure, but the spending is real and budgeted. He says gestures can cost two dollars or nothing, and some did cost real money (S001).

## Service versus hospitality (the core test)
- **Stance:** Service is the promise you sell; hospitality is how people feel while you deliver it. His test is whether the guest feels seen. Sources: S002, S003, S007, S011, S014, S015, S016, S018 (8).
- "service is black and white. Hospitality is color." (S002, 2022)
- "Service is simply a part of the product you’re selling." (S011, 2024-07)
- "It's hospitality. If they feel seen." (S014, 2024-11)

## Excellence as the floor
- **Stance:** Never pitch hospitality on a broken operation. Excellence is required, and it earns you nothing extra. Sources: S001, S014, S017 (3).
- "my entire thesis is that excellence is merely table stakes." (S001, 2025-03)
- Excellence and hospitality pull against each other; fear cultures get precision without humanity, all-praise cultures get warmth with sloppy detail, and success lives in the tension (S017, 2026-05).

## Culture
- **Stance:** The leader defines culture, and it has to be said out loud and tended every day. Trust given produces trustworthiness. Sources: S001, S003, S004, S005, S011, S014, S016, S017, S018, S019 (10).
- "I found that the more trust you give people, the more trustworthy they become." (S004, 2023)
- "It needs to be tended to and fed and watered and nurtured." (S018, 2022)
- "I think making good things mandatory is a beautiful thing for a culture" (S019, 2026-05)
- Yelling kitchens come from immature leaders (S001). Fix the cause of low morale, not a happy hour (S015). Short phrases (isms) and renamed roles signal what matters (S003, S017).

## Leadership
- **Stance:** Leading is an act of hospitality toward your own team. Say non-negotiables, be consistent, praise in public, criticize in private, and never in anger. Sources: S001, S004, S005, S011, S014, S016, S017, S018 (8).
- "I believe hospitality is the new, or will soon be the new required leadership skill." (S005, 2023-04)
- "praise is affirmation. Criticism is investment." (S011, 2024-07; also S017, 2026-05)
- "Criticize the behavior, not the person." (S011, 2024-07)
- "you should always make decisions from a place of strength and hope, not weakness and fear." (S011, 2024-07)
- Do the menial work yourself; the higher you are, the more it matters (S014).

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire for chemistry and character over credentials; keep the must-have list short; interview like a first date. Sources: S001, S004, S005, S015, S017, S018 (6).
- "We over-index on trying to hire for capacity and under-index on trying to hire for chemistry." (S001, 2025-03)
- "I try to pick the right people, not the best people" (S004, 2023)
- His four questions: can I trust them, will they work hard, do I want to spend time with them, will my people (S001). His best interview question was the difference between service and hospitality (S015, S018).
- **Over time:** as a young manager he ran interviews from a list of questions (S018, 2022, describing earlier years); now he says to hold a real conversation (S018, 2022; S017, 2026-05, newer).

## Training
- **Stance:** Both service and hospitality can be taught, best by letting staff receive hospitality first. Training never stops after onboarding. Sources: S001, S002, S011, S014, S015, S017 (6).
- "I think you can train both." (S015, 2022-10)
- "I don't think it's possible to know how good it feels to give it until you first have experienced how good it feels to receive it." (S014, 2024-11)
- He rejects the common line "hire for hospitality, train for excellence" (S004, S015).

## Scaling a feeling across locations
- **Stance:** You cannot touch every guest, but you can touch every employee, so invest in staff and use the daily pre-shift meeting to repeat what you stand for. Put systems behind the magic so it does not depend on one passionate person. Sources: S001, S011, S014, S016, S017, S018, S019 (7).
- "the idea that 30 minutes a day can transform a culture" (S018, 2022)
- He could reach maybe 4% of guests himself but 100% of staff (S011, 2024-07).
- Systems: the Dreamweaver role (S001, S002, S004, S007, S014), one size fits all, some, one (S017, S019), pre-planned responses to recurring moments (S017, S019).
- **Over time:** 2022-10: he called the hot dog a one-off gesture that takes real energy (S015). 2024-11 (newer): he says the hot dog moment was then systemized into a repeatable program (S014).

## Fine dining versus casual
- **Stance:** Hospitality belongs to any business that chooses it, from In-N-Out to a dentist. Sources: S002, S003, S011, S014, S015, S017, S018 (7).
- He wanted EMP to be the most casual version of luxury and took the same ideas to a MoMA cafeteria (S017, 2026-05).
- He loves Taco Bell and says you do not love food if you judge what others love (S014, 2024-11).
- **Changed view:** he moved from more is more to less is more (S003, 2023).

## Firing and poor performers
- **Stance:** Give a struggling person everything first, try a better role, then let go without guilt. Sources: S005 (1).
- "hire slow, fire fast, but not too fast" (S005, 2023-04)
- "You can't identify a poor performer until you've given all of yourself to make them great." (S005, 2023-04)

## Customer recovery and complaints
- **Stance:** A fixed failure makes your most loyal customers; fund it. Choose connection over being right. Sources: S017, S018 (2).
- "I think of customer recovery as one of the greatest forms of marketing you can do, and people woefully underspend on it." (S017, 2026-05)
- "it's not about who's right or who's wrong, it's about how you made someone feel" (S018, 2022)

## Technology and AI
- **Stance:** Use technology to make people more human, not to remove them. Sources: S003, S004, S005, S014, S017, S019 (6).
- **Over time:**
  - 2023: "some companies will use AI to give humans the tools to be even more human, and some companies will use AI to replace their humans." (S004) He fears the second.
  - 2026-05 (newer): he accepts some cuts. Company A cuts 20% and replaces with AI; Company B cuts 10%, uses AI for the same functions, and redeploys the rest to human work. B wins; A is "making today dollars at the expense of tomorrow" (S017).
- Hybrid work and AI raise the need for connection (S005). The table is one of few places people put phones down (S014).

## Reviews and rankings
- **Stance:** Use rankings as fuel and a tool, not an identity. Sources: S002, S003, S005, S014, S015, S017 (6).
- Coming last on the 50 Best list demoralized him, then gave him the goal of being number one for hospitality (S002, S015). Accolades are important and dangerous (S005, 2023-04).
- "We always lead with gratitude that people were trusting us" (S015, 2022-10)
- 2024-11: he says he should be better at being more complacent (S014). 2026-05: a review saying EMP needed more Miles Davis became a north star for constant reinvention (S017).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** No recorded position on price levels or menu pricing. Nearest: do not police how guests eat (S002), and he opposes price-goes-up-tomorrow urgency tactics; an honest renewal warning is hospitality even if some cancel (S019, 2026-05).

## Burnout and capacity
- **Stance:** Days off and pay treat symptoms; meaningful work treats the cause. Sources: S005, S014, S017, S018 (4).
- More time off and money are symptom fixes (S005, 2023-04). He admits he says yes too much and has less margin since having kids (S017, 2026-05), and is in no rush on his next project with a newborn (S018, 2022).
- "adversity is a terrible thing to waste" (S005, 2023-04, his father's line)

## Selling a restaurant
- **Stance:** No recorded position on how or when to sell. Facts only: he sold EMP at the start of 2020 while it was ranked number one (S007), and says being bought out plus having a baby helped him let go of an identity built on the restaurant (S015, 2022-10). He was a week from signing three new leases when Covid hit (S015).

## Founder topics

## Competition
- **Stance:** Products and brands get matched; relationships built through hospitality are the only lasting edge. Sources: S001, S003, S004, S007, S011, S016, S017 (7).
- "The only competitive advantage that exists over the long term comes through hospitality, through consistently investing in relationships" (S011, 2024-07)
- "I believe we are on the precipice of becoming a hospitality economy." (S007, 2022-09)
- Win on a touchpoint rivals ignore (S004). Borrow from the best, then add your own (S003).

## Metrics and ROI
- **Stance:** Hospitality is hard to measure, which is why companies drop it; do it anyway. Sources: S011, S016, S017, S018, S019 (5).
- What gets measured gets managed, and he offers no metric (S011). He claims it makes businesses more profitable but gives no hard numbers (S017, S018). Do things because they are right even when ROI cannot be measured, and reject vanity metrics (S019, 2026-05).

## Downturns and adversity
- **Stance:** Tighten defense but keep playing offense with creativity. Sources: S016, S018 (2).
- "no team, when they are losing, ever wins if they only play defense" (S016, 2024-09)

## Marketing, content and brand
- **Stance:** Hospitality is marketing; skip schlocky growth tactics and sell at the end of something that teaches. Sources: S001, S004, S016, S017, S019 (5).
- He is fine booking hospitality spend as marketing (S001). A brand should not forbid things that make people happy (S004). No growth hacks or tricked engagement (S019).
- "how you say goodbye is just as important as how you say hello" (S019, 2026-05)

## Partners and first hires
- **Stance:** Start with people you trust and test the fit before committing. Sources: S004, S017, S019 (3).
- Start with someone you trust, not strangers, and expect tension among passionate partners (S004). He worked with Brian Canlis for a year as a test, friends first (S019).

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Grow from your natural gift. Sources: S019 (1). He built his business around hosting in person, in four buckets (speaking and books, gatherings, workshops, creative studio). He left consulting because he cared too much and ideas got watered down (S019, 2026-05).

## Remote work
- **Stance:** Thin. Hybrid work raises the need for deliberate connection; he names short video check-ins (S005, 2023-04).
